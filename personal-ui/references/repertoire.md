# Repertoire

The owner's collection is at `desktop/www/repertoire/design`. Selected rendered pages and their source PDFs travel with this skill, so useful examples remain available on another computer. Open `originals/` for the complete PDFs used by the examples below. Unused PDFs and editable Figma sources stay in the owner's collection.

The filenames below use one-based PDF page numbers. [examples/index.json](examples/index.json) maps every bundled image to its source. These are personal design references, not assets to redistribute in an app. Open a few relevant images, then apply the principle to the current task.

## Dark hierarchy and controls

[Cloak's project manager](examples/cloak/projects.png) is the owner's current approved product example. It was captured from the real Windows app with isolated test repositories. Use it to study compact utility layout and restrained accent placement, then adapt those principles to the current product. The live implementation is in [zF4ke/cloak](https://github.com/zF4ke/cloak).

For setup work, inspect [Cloak onboarding](examples/cloak/onboarding.png) and [its installer](examples/cloak/setup.png). The onboarding shows connected phases, a fitted form and grouped navigation. The installer shares the app's controls and colors. These captures are from the 0.2.0 release candidate, not proposed mockups.

- [Colors That Ruin, page 4](examples/colors-that-ruin-p4.png) shows a black canvas, quiet grouped metadata and one purple object illustration. Transfer the neutral hierarchy. Its tall mobile card layout is not Cloak's layout.
- [Colors That Ruin, page 1](examples/colors-that-ruin-p1.png) provides the related earlier cloud-storage composition for comparison.
- [Software colors, page 4](examples/software-colors-p4.png) is a neutral value progression. Use value to establish hierarchy before adding color.
- [Software colors, page 1](examples/software-colors-p1.png) and [page 17](examples/software-colors-p17.png) provide wider source context.

## Dense information and progressive disclosure

- [Dashboard UI, page 1](examples/dashboard-ui-p1.png) and [page 5](examples/dashboard-ui-p5.png) show information layouts. Borrow alignment and grouping only where the product actually has that data.
- [Micro Dashboard, page 2](examples/micro-dashboard-p2.png) and [page 3](examples/micro-dashboard-p3.png) show task rows and task details. Notice the relationship between a list and selected details rather than filling a utility with unrelated widgets.
- [Dashboard Flaws, page 1](examples/dashboard-flaws-p1.png) shows the table context. [Page 8](examples/dashboard-flaws-p8.png) is a focused contextual explanation. Disclose specialist information close to its trigger.
- [Every UI Concept, page 1](examples/every-ui-concept-p1.png), [page 7](examples/every-ui-concept-p7.png) and [page 13](examples/every-ui-concept-p13.png) provide varied product compositions. Choose a fitting pattern rather than treating the PDF as a universal app shell.

## Expressivity and different product types

- [Micro-Animations, page 13](examples/micro-animations-p13.png), [page 25](examples/micro-animations-p25.png) and [page 45](examples/micro-animations-p45.png) provide visual frames. They show possible hierarchy and expressive objects, not proven animation timings.
- [Swipe Anims, page 1](examples/swipe-anims-p1.png) and [page 3](examples/swipe-anims-p3.png) support gesture or transition exploration. Check the original sequence when implementing it.
- [Kill boring designs, page 1](examples/kill-boring-designs-p1.png) and [page 7](examples/kill-boring-designs-p7.png) are useful for a promotional or expressive product. They do not justify promotional decoration inside a file-management utility.
- [Software Sections, page 1](examples/software-sections-p1.png), [page 4](examples/software-sections-p4.png) and [page 12](examples/software-sections-p12.png) illustrate section and menu composition.
